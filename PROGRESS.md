## Themes — status (as of 2026-09-11)

Three switchable skins, same data and layouts. Chosen from the menu on `/login` (and the three
swatches in the sidebar / mobile header), persisted in `localStorage` under `quest.theme`, applied
to `<html data-theme>` by an inline script before the first paint so there is no flash. Everything
a theme owns is one `[data-theme]` block in `src/theme/tokens.css` plus its entry in
`src/theme/themes.ts`: colour tokens (all colour in the app now goes through them — no raw Tailwind
palette classes remain), type pairing (self-hosted latin subsets via `@fontsource`, only the active
theme's faces download), textures (`body::before`), motion language (scale, easing, page-transition
preset, particle recipe) and an icon dialect (per-theme Lucide mapping + stroke style).

all honour reduced motion (Playwright asserts the particle layer is absent under it).

### My ranking

1. **Iron Crown.** The strongest fit for what the app is: a ledger dressed as a war room. Dark
   stone gives the gold counters and ember accents the most contrast per pixel, so the numbers —
   the point of every screen — read first and the decoration second. Cinzel's heavy caps carry the
   fantasy tone without hurting Crimson Pro's legibility in dense tables, and the slow, deliberate
   motion matches the cadence of a chronicle rather than a stock ticker. It also needed the fewest
   theme-specific fixes in its POLISH loop (all six were layout issues shared by every skin), which
   tells me its tokens are the most robust. It stays the default.
2. **Neon Kingdom.** The most *legible* of the three for scanning data: monospace numerals line up
   every column, the fast HUD motion makes navigation feel instant, and the black-glass cards with
   bracket corners are genuinely striking on the Throne Room and Oracle. It ranks second because it
   drifts from "fantasy strategy" toward "sci-fi HUD", and because its type has less headroom —
   Rajdhani is condensed and needed size nudges, Inter is wide and wraps labels that fit elsewhere.
   Violet-and-gold on black is also the most fatiguing of the three over a long session.
3. **Gilded Realm.** The most distinctive and the one people will screenshot: drop caps, fleurons,
   a page-turn transition and a hero counter that looks engraved. It ranks last on fitness, not
   beauty: a light scheme fights the brief's glow language (glow on cream had to become an engraved
   shadow; the particles had to be recoloured from motes to gold dust), gold-on-parchment contrast
   is inherently tight so several accents run darker than the palette wants, and the Recharts
   fills are less vivid. It is the best skin for daylight reading and printing, and the one I would
   show a sponsor.

## Exodus — Step 1: the lot ledger (`src/domain/lotLedger.ts`, parity pinned in `lotLedger.test.ts`)

`computeLotLedger(farmId, source, asOf)` is a pure port of Payments' `compute_lot_ledger(p_farm_id,
p_as_of)`: rate in PERCENT from `farm_acquisitions.annual_interest_rate`, lots from `properties`,
cost events from every `property_costs` row (lot-level to the lot, farm-level split equally among
the lots not yet released that day, skipped when none), credit events from note down payments on
`start_date`, `note_sales` on `sale_date` and completed cash cases on `closing_date` when the lot
has no note; costs before credits on the same day; a credit on a released lot is residual; otherwise
credits grow, `balance = capital + interest to that day − credits`, and `balance ≤ 0` releases the
lot with credits capped at the balance. `outstandingAt(lot, date)` runs an unreleased lot forward
(this is the partial-release cost Exodus will use). `computeLotLedgers(source, asOf, dealType)`
builds one ledger per farm of a deal type (fixed_interest by default).

`scripts/snapshot.ts` now also calls the RPC (read-only) for every fixed-interest farm and stores
the raw rows under `lotLedgers`; the fixture was regenerated at 19:42 UTC (only Avery Lot 4's stage
moved since 19:17, no pinned number reads it). **Parity: 49 lots on 6 farms (Eastland 11, Titus 6,
Freestone 7, Avery 14, Franklin 6, Franklin 2 5) match on capital, accrued interest, credits,

### How the engine works (`src/domain/exodus.ts`, pure; reuses `goal.ts`, `oracle.ts`, `pipeline.ts`, `warplan.ts` and `lotLedger.ts`)

`prepareExodus` (once per deadline) solves the **War Plan in cash mode** for the LP capital — its
closings per month, its partner-funded farms and their landing months, its ad spend — and replays the
Oracle's inventory consumption: today's 83 unsold lots first (shared among the nine farms that still
have lots by what each has left), then each new farm's lots as they land. `runExodus` (cheap; the
slider, the `maxNotesPct` scan and the 0 % baseline all call it) runs the monthly loop from
2026-09-11 to the deadline: **1** closings per the plan, down payments through each farm's
waterfall; **2** the best release candidates the target still needs are reserved, every other note
that is due is sold at the real ratio; **3** ads are paid; **4** allocation while delivered value <
target — free notes first (earliest first), then partial releases and own-cash farms ranked by
**settlement per dollar** with ties to the earliest date (`compareAllocationOptions`); nothing at or
below 1.0× is bought because a cash dollar settles a dollar; **5** Option A — once the target is met
every note still held and due is sold; on the last day everything left is sold; **6** every remaining
Portafolio dollar goes to the LPs. Feasible when notes + cash ≥ the LP capital by the deadline.
Eligibility: `own_capital` notes are deliverable as they are; `fixed_interest` notes only once the
lot's outstanding is paid (a partial release priced by the lot ledger run forward to that month —
`lotClaimAt`); `profit_share` notes never (always sold); excluded codes never (neither delivered nor
sold). UPB amortizes monthly, `upb × (1 + rate/12) − payment`, floored at 0; a delivery is valued at
the UPB of its month. Existing notes' lots are released whole; the delivered fraction of the last
note is whatever the target still needs and the remainder is sold the same month. Projected batches
(one farm's closings in one month) are divisible.

### Every assumption (each also in `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`, #81–#96)

1. **Note-sale ratio 80.92 % ("combined")** — per sale, `sale_price ÷` the unpaid balance implied by
   `discount_from_upb` when it is recorded (6 sales, 82.75 %), else `÷ notes.financed_amount` (all 15,
   80.45 %). The brief's literal `sale_price ÷ (sale_price + discount_from_upb)` reads 99.98 % because
   `discount_from_upb` is a percent; it is shown and not used (#81). Editable on the page.
2. **Starting cash $0.** Payments holds no bank balance. The War Plan's cash mode starts from cash
   kept − owed today ($1,362,503.84 − $4,801,191.96 = −$3,438,688.12), a position, not a balance;
   both figures are shown beside the input and the bridge explains the difference (#82).
3. **Production = the War Plan's cash-mode required plan** for the same target and deadline
   (20.53 closings/month, 259.36 lots, 18 farms, $68,433/month of ads). At 0 % the Exodus closes the
   same lots, buys the same farms and spends the same ads; the War Plan ↔ Exodus **cash bridge** is
   explained to the cent (start position $3,438,688.12 · receipts −$77,147.76 · existing notes
   +$1,034,918.91 · partner payments −$1,136,076.03 · ads −$864,541.07 · replay drift −$202.42 ·
   residual $0) (#83).
4. **Committed capital counts from day one.** Franklin 2's ledger costs are dated 2026-10-15; its
   capital is a claim on its lots already (interest only from that date). Lakeview's $495,000 is
   Townson capital to return from its lots from day one although `funding_date` is null (#84).
5. **13 farm-less notes ($1,031,429.19) and the excluded EAS-L04 ($42,050.43) are neither delivered
   nor sold**; they are listed under "No farm" / "Excluded" and produce no cash in the model (#85).
6. **A War Plan farm is classified by its largest funding slice**; unfunded capital counts as fixed
   interest at the mix's first fixed rate (Kevin Concua's 20 %). On this data: 13 fixed-interest
   farms (releasable), 5 Townson farms (never delivered — including Farm 3, Kevin $151,288 + Townson
   $317,232), 0 own-capital farms (#86).
7. **Profit-share waterfall**: a lot's proceeds return Townson's capital on that farm first, then split
   at the farm's share (50 %); the Oracle/War Plan books Townson's take as gross × 50 % from the first
   dollar. The difference lives in the bridge's "partner payments" (#87).
8. **Unsold fixed-interest lots at the deadline** (3.64 lots, $193,280.12 at 30 %) keep their partner
   balance; it is reported, not paid (#88).
9. **Ads are paid from Portafolio cash** before anything reaches the LPs, exactly the War Plan's
   `adSpendPerClosing × closings` (#89).
10. **Future notes use real averages**: face $117,504.74 (= avg sale price $127,335 − 7.72 % down),
    9.46 % (mean rate of the realm's 39 farm notes), 140 months, $1,388.87/month; they are sold, if
    not delivered, `noteSaleLagMonths` (3) after closing. Existing notes use their own `current_upb`,
    `interest_rate` (fraction), `monthly_payment`, and are sold at `start_date + 3 months` or in the
    first month if that is past (#90).
11. **Own-cash farms** are War Plan farms bought with Portafolio cash instead of the plan's funding
    (then every note they produce is free); viable only when `purchaseMonth ≤ deadline − land lag −
    close lag` (Jul 2027). On this data none is ever chosen: a farm is bought whole in its purchase
    month and Portafolio never holds $468,520 then; once the target is met nothing is bought (#91).
12. **`maxNotesPct` = 60 is the slider's ceiling**, not a binding limit: the inventory covers every
    percent up to 60 (#92).
13. **Lots not needed = 0 at 30 %** because both the 30 % plan (Oct 5, 2027) and the baseline
    (Oct 23, 2027) finish after the plan's last closing month (Sep 2027 in the War Plan), so all 259.36
    lots are sold either way; at 60 % it becomes 8.28 (#93).
14. **Replay drift −$202.42** between the Oracle's cash metric and the replayed consumption (16
    rounded rows, $27M of receipts); the test tolerates $250 and pins the residual at $0 (#94).
15. **Any note on a lot blocks the cash-closing credit**, and the unnumbered Eastland property is a lot
    (inherited from the ledger port, #80); existing notes' lots are released whole (#95).
16. **Live Payments moved twice on 2026-09-11** (19:17 → 19:42 → 21:15–21:17 UTC); the fixture is the
    21:31 UTC state and the live page reproduced it to the dollar at 22:11 UTC (#96).

## Access — Payments staff only (2026-09-11; `src/domain/access.ts`, `src/data/auth.tsx`, `src/data/queries` `fetchOwnProfile`; e2e "Access — Payments staff only"; live at https://terrafunded-quest-v2.vercel.app/login)

**Rule.** After sign-in Quest reads the signed-in user's **own** `profiles` row — columns `id, role`
only, `.eq("id", <auth user id>).limit(1)` — and admits the user only when **`role = 'admin'`**. Any

**signed out of this browser immediately** and `/login` shows **"Quest is for the TerraFunded team
only."** The one exception is by e-mail: the questbot `viewer` that `npm run e2e` and `npm run
verify:live` sign in with, named in the build-time env var **`QUEST_ALLOWED_TEST_EMAIL`** (set in

*"Read-only, and for the TerraFunded team only: Payments staff (role admin) may enter; nothing here

**Where it lives.** `decideAccess()` (pure, `src/domain/access.ts`, 15 unit tests: admin wins
regardless of e-mail; the allowed e-mail wins case-insensitively and trimmed; empty/unset allow-list
is never a wildcard; `Admin`/`ADMIN`/`' admin'`/`NULL`/missing row refused). `AuthProvider` runs the
read once per page load per user (token refreshes re-use the verdict) and **derives** `access`

renders the skeleton while checking and nothing but a redirect otherwise; `useRealm` is disabled until
`granted`, so a refused user triggers exactly one request (`profiles`) and no realm read. A profile
that cannot be read fails **closed** with the sentence plus the error text. The refused sign-out is
`scope: "local"` (this browser only) so it cannot revoke the account's other sessions.

## Exit horizon — 2027 / 2028 / 2029

The founder can switch the whole app between three exit years. No free date picker at the app
level; War Plan and Exodus keep their own scenario deadline fields.

**What it is.** `GOAL_DEADLINE` is no longer a single hard-wired day the pages read. It remains
`deadlineForHorizon(2027)` (`"2027-12-31"`) as the fallback inside `computeGoal` only. The rest of
the runtime reads `ctx.goal.deadline`, which `buildRealm` now accepts as `RealmOptions.deadline`.
The three years are `ExitHorizon = 2027 | 2028 | 2029` in `src/config/goal.ts`.

**Persistence.** `src/horizon/horizon.ts` + `HorizonProvider` follow the ThemeProvider pattern:
storage key `quest.v2.exitHorizon`, `readStoredHorizon()` validates against `EXIT_HORIZONS` and
falls back to 2027 on garbage, a `storage` listener for other tabs, try/catch around `setItem`.
The choice survives route changes, reloads, and logout/login in the same browser. Sign-out does
not clear it.

**No Payments refetch.** `useRealm` caches the raw `PaymentsSnapshot` under `REALM_QUERY_KEY` and
builds the realm in a `useMemo` keyed on `(snapshot, deadline)`. Switching years is instant and
hits Supabase zero times. `asOf` stays the fetch-time clock.

**What moves / what does not.** Required, projected, at-the-deadline and by-`<date>` figures
recompute (days left, required lots/month, required reservations/month, net profit required per
day, War Plan / Exodus defaults, Oracle before/after-deadline verdicts, capital owed at the
deadline, Throne "days to" copy, cinematic intro year). Historical facts do not: net profit to
date, cash realized, live reservations, capital owed *today*, interest accrued to date, trophy
*count*, chronicle events. `goal.farmsStillNeeded` is inventory-gap ÷ avg lots and does not read
the deadline (existing math, unchanged) — the War Plan's last purchase date is what slides later.
Unit test: `src/domain/__tests__/horizon.test.ts`.

**UI.** Drawer section **HORIZONTE · EXIT** at the top of the scrollable nav (above Throne Room),
segmented 2027 | 2028 | 2029, gold border on the current year, caption in the current lang.
Selecting a year does not close the drawer. Topbar reads `Exodus · <year>` (`data-testid="topbar-horizon"`),
13px on a 380px viewport so it still fits next to the oxygen pill. Horizon changes do not replay
the cinematic intro or SinceLastVisit celebrations.

**War Plan / Exodus.** Their default/"real" deadline is the global horizon. Changing the horizon
while the page is open re-seeds inputs from the new defaults. A saved scenario keeps the deadline
it was saved with and does not write the global horizon.

**The Pulse.** Under the Throne Room net-profit number, above the pace line: PRODUCING =
`oxygen.netProfitPerDayAtPace` (today's trailing pace — does not move with the horizon) and
NEEDED = `debt.requiredNetProfitPerDay` (remaining ÷ days left — does). The ratio line reads
"you are at N% of the pace the <year> horizon requires", ember under 90%, gold 90–110%,
oxygen above 110%. Still shown on the Debt card; this is a promotion, not a move. Oxygen card
copy: "today the realm produces $X of net profit per day" / "hoy el reino produce $X de
utilidad neta al día"; heading is "cumulative days already gained".
